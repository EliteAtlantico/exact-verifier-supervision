**Title.** Perfect Labels for Free? What Exact Answers and Exact Traces Teach a Small Language Model

**TL;DR.** With labels from exact verifiers, answer-only fine-tuning learns surface shortcuts and, at 3B and 7B, removes from the answers a division the base model already performs, while procedure traces teach the steps within a task once they are supervised often enough; timestamped predictions, controls and exact audits, with every failed prediction reported.

**Abstract.** (keep identical to the abstract in main.tex; regenerate after the final build)

Exact verifiers (primality tests, SAT solvers, unit tests) label data without noise and supply the rewards of verifiable-reward post-training. Because every label is exact, a difference between two training regimes on the same inputs is a difference in the supervision format. We fine-tune Qwen2.5 models (0.5B to 7B; main size 1.5B, three seeds) with LoRA on exactly labeled decision tasks and compare supervision by the answer alone with a deterministic procedure trace followed by the answer. Three findings. Exact answers teach shortcuts: on 4-digit primality the answers follow "the last digit is 1, 3, 7 or 9" on 99.2-100.0% of test items, and on divisibility by 7 or 13 answer-only training stays at chance at every training size we ran, including a sweep over learning rates and epochs, logged before it ran, in which none of eight answer-only runs collapses and none leaves chance while the same recipe learns divisibility by 3. Exact answers suppress computation: at 3B and 7B the zero-shot model already divides by 7 (94.2% and 82.5%); answer-only fine-tuning drops it to chance in both seeds at each size, while fine-tuning on the model's own verifier-filtered traces keeps it (3B, two seeds; 7B, one seed). Removing the intermediate tokens removes serial computation by construction; asked for a chain of thought, the answer-only model at 1.5B no longer writes one, so the behaviour is suppressed and whether the capability underneath survives is untested. Exact traces teach steps seen often enough: within a task, accuracy and per-step accuracy rise with the number of training traces and, at the entry level, with how often that exact table entry was supervised; a count-based prediction across tasks, logged before its cells ran, failed because steps differ in difficulty, and traces are not uniformly better (at 1.5B they hurt on primality). Controls show that the trace must be the input's own computation, written before the answer: a scrambled trace, a correct trace of another number, and a trace placed after the answer all stay at chance (two seeds each). In a second model family (SmolLM2-1.7B-Instruct), traces beat answers on divisibility by 7 in both seeds and the grounding control stays at chance, but the gain misses the 20-point threshold we had set in one seed. Four of twelve logged predictions failed and are reported, and exact audits show trace-trained models writing unfaithful steps behind right answers. Verified labels do not by themselves determine what a model learns; the supervision format and the base model's own computation do.
